itgraph

A private analytics tool that follows a hand-reviewed set of IT channels and flags the videos and posts their audiences are responding to unusually strongly right now.

Status, November 2026. itgraph has been running for public Telegram channels since summer 2026. The YouTube part is being built on the same approach: the channel inventory, the tracking of video statistics, the scores that raise YouTube alerts and the delivery of alerts are in use; the weekly lists are being added.

What it does with YouTube

  • A reviewed inventory. A list of channels about software development and IT, mostly English-language, with a small number in Russian. A person looks at each channel and labels it: what kind of channel it is, which language it publishes in, and whether it is in scope.
  • Public statistics of new videos. For channels in scope, itgraph reads the public view, like and comment counts of recent uploads several times as each video ages.
  • Past uploads. For channels in scope, itgraph also lists up to a year of earlier uploads and reads their titles and descriptions. It does not read their view, like or comment counts.
  • Mentions. From the titles and descriptions of those channels' uploads, new and past, itgraph works out which other channels and videos they mention or link to — to map how the channels refer to one another, and to find channels to review. It works this out when it is needed and keeps no copy. When a mentioned or linked channel or video is not in the inventory, itgraph asks YouTube which channel it belongs to, and nothing else.
  • Featured channels. For channels in scope, itgraph reads which other channels each features on its channel page — to find channels to review.
  • Search. itgraph searches YouTube for recent videos and channels about software development, with search terms the developer chooses, and keeps only which channel each result belongs to — to find channels to review.
  • Links from Telegram. When posts of the public Telegram channels itgraph follows link to YouTube channels or videos, itgraph asks YouTube which channel each linked video, handle or name belongs to, and nothing else — to find channels to review. It keeps that answer for at most 30 days and works out the rest when it is needed.
  • Alerts. When a video is doing much better than that channel's own videos usually do at the same age, itgraph sends a short message to a private Telegram chat for YouTube alerts, in a topic for the channel's language, English or Russian: the video's title and channel, a link to it on YouTube, the counts YouTube reports with the time they were read, and itgraph's score with the time it was calculated. Channels in other languages get no alerts. A message stays in the chat as it was sent.
  • Weekly lists. Every Monday, itgraph sends to each language's topic a list of up to ten channels whose videos published the previous week did best against that channel's own videos, one video per channel, each with the same content as an alert. A list record holds IDs, itgraph's scores and when it was sent; an entry is deleted together with the reading it was calculated on.

itgraph's score is calculated independently by itgraph. It is not a YouTube metric, it is labelled as itgraph's own wherever it appears, and it is always shown next to the counts YouTube reports.

How it uses the YouTube API Services

  • The YouTube Data API v3, authenticated with an API key. Public data only: itgraph never signs in to a YouTube or Google account, does not use OAuth, and does not access anyone's private data.
  • Methods: channels.list for the inventory, to notice new uploads, and to find which channel a linked handle belongs to; playlistItems.list and videos.list for new and past uploads, and for the statistics of new ones; videos.list also to find which channel a linked video belongs to; search.list and channelSections.list for finding channels to review.
  • Data itgraph stores from YouTube is refreshed or deleted within 30 days and is never included in backups. What itgraph keeps beyond that are its own records: channel IDs and the reviewer's labels. Alert messages already sent stay in the chat as they were sent. The Privacy Policy has the details.

Access

itgraph is not a public service and has no sign-up. Its alerts go to private Telegram chats whose members are invited by the developer. The source code is public on GitHub.
